# Founder-Readability Law — a repo contract

**Plain English — what this is:** a binding rule for *every* report, gate card, and document in this
repository. FounderGate serves a non-developer founder. A document that a founder cannot act on is not done,
no matter how technically correct it is.

**Why it matters:** the whole product promise is that a non-developer can run senior-quality development. That
promise dies the moment our own docs assume a developer reader. This law is how we keep the promise in writing.

**What can go wrong:** jargon-first reports, buried decisions, "here are 6 options" with no recommendation, or a
document that ends without telling the founder what to do next. Each of those breaks the contract.

---

## The contract

Every report, gate card, and doc in this repo **must lead with plain English** and, at the top, answer these
five things in order:

1. **What this is** — one or two sentences, no jargon.
2. **Why it matters** — the stakes in founder terms.
3. **What can go wrong** — the honest risks, not just the happy path.
4. **What the founder can test now** — a concrete thing to run, look at, or check.
5. **Exactly one next action** — a single, specific next step. Not a menu.

### Rules of application

- **Plain English leads.** Technical detail may follow, but never before the five points above.
- **Exactly one next action.** If there are genuinely multiple paths, the author picks a recommended one and
  says so; the founder is not a router.
- **Honest sourcing.** Claims of "done" must point to proof (a green self-test in this repo, a linked artifact).
  No proof, no "done."
- **The founder is not a prompt relay.** Docs do not ask the founder to pass messages between systems; they ask
  the founder to make decisions only a human owner can make.

### Applies to

Reports · gate cards · decision records · extraction map & ledger entries · PR bodies · doctrine docs · READMEs.

## What the founder can test now

Open any doc in `docs/`. If its first lines don't answer the five questions above, it is out of compliance —
tell the operator and it gets fixed.

## One next action

When reviewing any FounderGate output, read only the top of the document first. If you know what to do after
those few lines, the law is being kept.
